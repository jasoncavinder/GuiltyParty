# Trademark Planning Notes

## Status

This document records internal naming and trademark-planning information only.
It is not legal advice, a trademark or common-law clearance opinion, or a
prediction of registration, protection, or enforceability.

This document does not state or imply that any project candidate or portfolio
name is available, registered to the project, registrable, protectable, or safe
to use. Do not add trademark or registration symbols without documented owner
approval following professional review. All numeric estimates are approximate
planning shorthand based on preliminary research, not legal conclusions.

## Naming Hierarchy

The current naming order is:

1. **Preferred working product name:** `Guilty Party`
2. **Primary fallback:** `Party in Question`
3. **Secondary fallback:** `Suspicious Party`

The fallback names are unadopted planning alternatives. They are not current
product names, co-brands, or evidence that any name is clear for use.

## Preferred Working Name: Guilty Party

`Guilty Party` remains the creatively preferred working product name because
of its strong double meaning:

- the guilty party is the person responsible for the mystery
- a party is also the social gathering where the experience occurs

Preliminary research identified meaningful complications:

- Disney released an exact-name `Guilty Party` multiplayer mystery/party video
  game.
- An active business called `Guilty Party Mysteries` provides hosted immersive
  murder-mystery events, closely adjacent to the product's initial market.

For internal planning only, treat the rough collision-risk estimate as
approximately **4/5**. That estimate is not a legal conclusion about confusion,
priority, availability, or permissible use.

## Ordered Contingency Names

### Primary Fallback: Party in Question

Reasons for retaining `Party in Question` as the primary fallback:

- It preserves the double meaning of "party" as both a gathering and a person
  involved in a matter.
- "In question" implies suspicion, investigation, and being the subject under
  consideration.
- Preliminary searching did not surface an obvious exact-name entertainment
  platform or established event company.
- It has a cleaner preliminary collision profile than the other alternatives.

That preliminary search observation is incomplete and time-sensitive. It is
not evidence of availability or clearance.

Approximate planning estimates:

- collision risk: **2/5**
- ownability or breadth of protection: **2–3/5**

The principal concern is that this is an ordinary legal and conversational
phrase, so any trademark protection may be relatively narrow. The estimate is
not a prediction of registrability, enforceability, or actual protection
breadth.

Possible provisional marketing line:

> Everyone is a party in question.

This is exploratory copy, not an adopted permanent tagline.

### Secondary Fallback: Suspicious Party

Reasons for retaining `Suspicious Party` as the secondary fallback:

- It is the closest tonal and structural replacement for `Guilty Party`.
- It retains the same concise two-word rhythm.
- It can describe both a suspicious person or group and an intriguing social
  gathering.
- It remains playful, theatrical, and mystery-oriented.

Approximate planning estimates:

- collision risk: **3/5**
- ownability or breadth of protection: **2/5**

An exact entertainment use exists in the fan-created game or mod title
`Friday Night Funkin': Suspicious Party`. The phrase also directly suggests a
mystery-party experience and may therefore receive narrower protection. These
are planning concerns, not conclusions about rights, registrability,
confusion, or permissible use.

## Deprioritized Candidate History

The following names were explored but are not active fallbacks:

- `Revel & Role` — broad and genre-flexible, but loses the investigative double
  meaning.
- `Cast Doubt` — strong theatrical and mystery wordplay, but a common idiom and
  likely difficult to own.
- `Act Suspicious` — memorable, but better suited to campaign copy, a scenario,
  or an event mode.
- `A Curious Gathering` — evocative and broad, but longer and less
  product-like.
- `Scene & Secret` — polished, but may sound like a theater company or
  production studio.
- `Acts & Alibis` and `Masks & Motives` — potentially useful for scenario
  collections or imprints rather than the platform.

contingency name or the provisional parent-company byline in current product or

that has not been authorized.

## Work Required Before Commercial Launch

Professional trademark and common-law clearance is required before a major
commercial launch or substantial branding investment.

Professional review should determine:

- availability and clearance in intended markets
- relevant goods and services classifications
- registration strategy and timing
- ownership and authorized-use rules
- treatment of creator, partner, and scenario branding
- domain and account-name conflicts

Search results, domain availability, and the planning estimates in this
document are not sufficient clearance.
